### `C-01` — Excessive Centralized Control and Honeypot Potential  *(Severity: Critical · Status: Unresolved)*

The contract owner possesses extensive control over critical token parameters, including the ability to modify buy and sell taxes (from 0% to 100%), update `_maxTxAmount` and `_maxWalletSize` (potentially to zero), enable/disable swaps, and exclude/include addresses from fees. This level of control allows the owner to effectively halt trading, prevent users from selling, or impose prohibitive taxes, creating a high risk of a rug pull or honeypot scenario where users can buy but not sell their tokens.

**Recommendation:** Implement a multi-signature wallet for ownership of critical functions. Introduce time-locks for changes to sensitive parameters like taxes and transaction limits. Consider decentralizing control over key parameters or implementing community governance for such changes.


### `H-01` — Extremely High Initial Taxes  *(Severity: High · Status: Unresolved)*

The contract is initialized with an `_initialBuyTax` of 15% and an `_initialSellTax` of 70%. These tax rates are exceptionally high and can lead to significant immediate losses for users, deterring legitimate trading and potentially trapping investors. While these taxes are designed to reduce over time, the initial rates are a major barrier to entry and exit.

**Recommendation:** Re-evaluate and significantly reduce initial tax percentages to foster a healthier trading environment and build user trust. Clearly communicate the tax structure and reduction schedule to all potential investors.


### `H-02` — Abusable Transfer Delay and Bot Exclusion Mechanisms  *(Severity: High · Status: Unresolved)*

The owner can enable `transferDelayEnabled` and arbitrarily add/remove addresses from the `bots` mapping. If `transferDelayEnabled` is active, users might be prevented from transferring tokens for a specified duration. Marking an address as a 'bot' can also restrict its ability to transfer. This functionality, while intended for anti-bot measures, can be abused by the owner to selectively block legitimate users from selling or moving their tokens, leading to a denial of service for specific addresses.

**Recommendation:** Remove the ability for the owner to arbitrarily mark addresses as bots or enable transfer delays. If such mechanisms are deemed necessary, implement transparent, auditable, and decentralized criteria for their activation and application, or remove them entirely to prevent abuse.


### `M-01` — Lack of Transparent Emergency Pause Mechanism  *(Severity: Medium · Status: Unresolved)*

While the owner has the ability to effectively halt transfers by setting `_maxTxAmount` to zero or taxes to 100%, there is no dedicated, explicit, and transparent pause/unpause mechanism. In an emergency, relying on parameter manipulation for a 'pause' can be less clear, potentially lead to unintended side effects, and lacks the clear signaling of a standard `Pausable` pattern.

**Recommendation:** Consider implementing a standard `Pausable` contract pattern (e.g., from OpenZeppelin) to provide a clear and explicit mechanism for pausing and unpausing token transfers in emergencies. This should ideally be controlled by a multi-signature wallet or a time-locked governance mechanism.


### `L-01` — Redundant SafeMath Library Usage  *(Severity: Low · Status: Unresolved)*

The contract uses the `SafeMath` library for arithmetic operations (add, sub, mul, div). However, the contract is compiled with Solidity version 0.8.19, which includes native overflow and underflow protection by default. The explicit use of `SafeMath` is therefore redundant and adds unnecessary code complexity without providing additional security benefits in this context.

**Recommendation:** Remove the `SafeMath` library and its `using SafeMath for uint256;` declaration. Rely on Solidity's native overflow/underflow checks for `uint256` operations in version 0.8.19 and above.


### `I-01` — Missing or Non-Standard SPDX License Identifier  *(Severity: Informational · Status: Unresolved)*

The contract uses `// SPDX-License-Identifier: NONE`. While technically valid, it is best practice to use a recognized and explicit open-source license identifier (e.g., MIT, GPL-3.0) to clearly define the terms under which the code can be used, modified, and distributed. Using 'NONE' can create ambiguity regarding intellectual property rights.

**Recommendation:** Replace `// SPDX-License-Identifier: NONE` with a standard and explicit open-source license identifier, such as `// SPDX-License-Identifier: MIT`, to improve clarity regarding the contract's licensing.
