### `H-01` — Centralized Control by Owner  *(Severity: High · Status: Unresolved)*

The contract grants extensive control to the `owner` (via `OwnableUpgradeable`) over critical parameters such as tax rates, tax durations, anti-farmer durations, liquidation thresholds, expected liquidation output, and the addresses of the `taxProcessor` and `dividendContract`. This high degree of centralization introduces significant governance and operational risk, as a compromised or malicious owner could unilaterally alter the token's economic behavior.

**Recommendation:** Implement a multi-signature wallet (e.g., Gnosis Safe) for the owner address. For highly sensitive functions, consider introducing a time-lock mechanism or a decentralized governance system to allow community oversight and reaction time before critical changes take effect.


### `H-02` — Reliance on External `taxProcessor` and `dividendContract`  *(Severity: High · Status: Unresolved)*

The contract relies on external `taxProcessor` and `dividendContract` addresses, which are set by the owner and can be changed. The `taxProcessor` is responsible for handling collected tax, and the `dividendContract` for share tracking. A malicious or compromised `taxProcessor` could potentially misappropriate collected tax funds, and a faulty `dividendContract` could disrupt dividend distribution or tracking. The security and trustworthiness of these external contracts are paramount and not within the scope of this audit.

**Recommendation:** Thoroughly audit and verify the security of the `taxProcessor` and `dividendContract` implementations. Consider implementing safeguards such as whitelisting trusted `taxProcessor` contracts, or adding mechanisms for users to withdraw their share of collected tax directly if the `taxProcessor` becomes unresponsive or malicious.


### `M-01` — Potential Storage Collision/Overflow Risk in `PackedPoolState`  *(Severity: Medium · Status: Unresolved)*

The `PackedPoolState` struct uses fixed-size types (`uint96`, `uint64`, `uint16`, `uint8`) packed into a single storage slot for gas efficiency. While `maxSupply` is currently a constant `1e9 ether` (1 billion tokens), the `liquidationThreshold` is `uint96`, which can hold up to approximately `79 * 10^9` tokens. The contract's `custom:security-note` correctly highlights that if the total supply limit were to increase beyond `79 * 10^9` tokens (with 18 decimals), `liquidationThreshold` could overflow. Although `maxSupply` is constant, future upgrades or forks might alter this, leading to potential issues if the `uint96` limit is exceeded.

**Recommendation:** Ensure that any future upgrades or modifications to the token's total supply or the `PackedPoolState` struct carefully consider the maximum values for each packed field, especially `liquidationThreshold`. If `maxSupply` is ever increased or if the `liquidationThreshold` could naturally grow beyond `uint96` limits, the type should be upgraded to `uint128` or `uint256` to prevent overflow.


### `M-02` — Incomplete Dynamic Liquidation Logic  *(Severity: Medium · Status: Unresolved)*

The contract description mentions 'Dynamic Tax Liquidation Threshold: The tax liquidation threshold is dynamically adjusted.' However, the provided code only exposes an `onlyOwner` function (`setLiquidationThreshold`) to explicitly set this value. There is no visible on-chain logic for automatic or dynamic adjustment based on market conditions or other parameters. This discrepancy suggests either missing logic in the provided code or a misleading description, which could lead to operational misunderstandings or unfulfilled expectations regarding the token's behavior.

**Recommendation:** Clarify the mechanism for 'dynamic adjustment' of the liquidation threshold. If the adjustment is intended to be manual by the owner, update the documentation to reflect this. If an automatic, on-chain dynamic adjustment mechanism is intended, implement and thoroughly test this logic, ensuring it is robust against manipulation.


### `L-01` — `block.timestamp` Reliance for Expiration  *(Severity: Low · Status: Unresolved)*

The `taxExpirationTime` and `antiFarmerExpirationTime` are calculated using `block.timestamp`. While `block.timestamp` is generally acceptable for time-based events, miners have a limited ability to manipulate it (typically within a few seconds of actual time). For expiration events, this manipulation window is usually not critical, but it's a known characteristic of `block.timestamp` that could be exploited in highly time-sensitive scenarios if the window of manipulation aligns with a critical state change.

**Recommendation:** For time-sensitive operations where miner manipulation of `block.timestamp` could have a significant impact, consider using an oracle-based timestamp or a time-weighted average oracle (e.g., Chainlink's `block.timestamp` feed) if available and suitable for the specific use case. For simple expiration, the current approach is generally acceptable.
