## Audit Summary

The DERC20 contract is a standard ERC-20 implementation with added governance, voting, and vesting capabilities. The protocol relies on a centralized owner to manage inflation rates and pool access. While the code follows standard OpenZeppelin patterns, the high level of administrative control over token supply and transferability presents a significant centralization risk to token holders.

> **Final Recommendation:** To improve security, it is recommended to transition the owner role to a multi-signature wallet or a decentralized governance contract. This would mitigate the risk of a single compromised key performing unauthorized minting or halting transfers.

Additionally, implementing a timelock for sensitive administrative functions (like updateMintRate) would provide transparency and allow the community to react to significant changes in tokenomics.

### `CP-01` — Centralized Control Over Token Supply  *(Severity: Medium · Status: Unresolved)*

The owner has the authority to call updateMintRate and release, which can increase the total supply of the token. This allows the owner to dilute the holdings of all existing token holders at their discretion.

**Recommendation:** Consider implementing a decentralized governance mechanism or a timelock to ensure that supply changes are transparent and community-approved.


### `CP-05` — Centralized Control Over Transferability  *(Severity: Medium · Status: Unresolved)*

The owner can use lockPool and unlockPool to toggle the transferability of tokens. This gives the owner the power to effectively freeze user funds or prevent trading at any time.

**Recommendation:** Restrict the ability to halt transfers to emergency scenarios only, and ideally, require a multi-signature consensus to trigger such a state change.
